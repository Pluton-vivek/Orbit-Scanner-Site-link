This Data Protection document describes the technical and organizational measures (TOMs) that PLUTON TECHNOLOGIES (OPC) PRIVATE LIMITED implements to protect personal data processed through Orbit Scanner. It supplements our Privacy Policy and provides additional transparency for users, business partners, regulators, and auditors who require detail on our security and data protection practices.

This document is maintained in accordance with the GDPR (Article 32), India DPDP Act, CCPA/CPRA, UK GDPR, LGPD, and ISO/IEC 27001-aligned principles.

1. Our Approach to Data Protection

Data protection is not an add-on feature at Orbit Scanner — it is an architectural choice. We design the App and our backend to collect the minimum data necessary, store data for the minimum time necessary, and protect that data with industry-standard controls.

Our principles:

  • Data minimization. We collect only what we need to provide and improve the service.
  • Purpose limitation. Data collected for one purpose is not repurposed without explicit user consent.
  • Storage limitation. We delete data as soon as it is no longer needed for its original purpose.
  • Integrity and confidentiality. Data is encrypted in transit and at rest.
  • Accountability. We maintain records of processing activities and respond to data subject requests within statutory timelines.
  • No data monetization. We do not sell data, share it for advertising, or train AI models on user content.

2. Ephemeral Cloud Processing

Our Architecture Choice

Documents you send to the cloud are deleted within 60 seconds.

When you use AI/OCR features (Layout OCR, Translate, To Word, Enhance, Extract Tables), your document is transmitted to our processing servers, processed by our AI/OCR provider, and deleted from our infrastructure immediately after the result is returned to you. We do not cache, log, archive, or retain copies. Our systems are designed to be incapable of long-term retention.

What this means in practice

  • Server-side delete is automatic. Storage used for processing has automatic lifecycle policies that delete objects after each job completes.
  • No content in logs. Server processing logs record operation type, duration, file size, and status code only. Document content, OCR output, and file bytes are never logged.
  • Sub-processors are contractually bound. Our AI/OCR provider operates under a Data Processing Agreement (DPA) that prohibits retention, training on customer data, or any secondary use.
  • On-device processing is preferred where possible. Edge detection, image cropping, and filters run entirely on-device.

Orbit Scanner does not currently offer a Cloud Sync feature. Scanned documents and signatures are stored locally on your device and are not uploaded to our servers except for the ephemeral AI/OCR processing described above.

3. Encryption Standards

3.1 Encryption in Transit

  • All network communication between the App and our backend uses TLS 1.2 or higher.
  • The Android app blocks cleartext traffic (android:usesCleartextTraffic="false").
  • Server-side TLS configuration disables deprecated protocols and weak ciphers.

3.2 Encryption at Rest

  • Backend data: Personal data stored on our backend is encrypted at rest using AES-256.
  • Cloud processing storage: Storage used for ephemeral AI/OCR processing uses server-side encryption (AES-256) and lifecycle policies that delete objects after each job.
  • On-device sensitive data: Authentication tokens and user profile cache are stored using AndroidX EncryptedSharedPreferences, with encryption keys managed by the platform’s hardware-backed keystore.
  • Scanned documents on device: Stored in app-private storage (sandboxed; not accessible to other apps without root).

3.3 Secrets Management

API keys, database credentials, and third-party tokens are not hardcoded in the App or backend source code. Secrets are stored as environment variables or in a dedicated secrets manager, and build-time secrets are injected via secured CI/CD pipelines.

4. Access Controls

  • Principle of least privilege. Backend services and personnel have access only to the systems and data they need to perform their function.
  • Multi-factor authentication. Access to production systems requires multi-factor authentication.
  • Key-based server access. Administrative access to production hosts uses individual keys rather than shared passwords.
  • Audit logging. Administrative actions on production systems are logged.
  • Environment separation. Development, staging, and production environments are isolated. Production data is not copied to development or staging.

5. Authentication & Sessions

  • Google Sign-In (OAuth 2.0). Primary authentication is via Google Sign-In using the OAuth 2.0 Authorization Code flow. Tokens are validated server-side against Google’s public keys. Minimal scopes are requested: openid, email, profile.
  • Password hashing. Where email/password authentication is offered, passwords are hashed using a strong, salted algorithm (bcrypt) before storage. Plaintext passwords are never stored.
  • Access tokens. Short-lived access tokens (JWTs) signed using strong keys.
  • Refresh tokens. Refresh tokens are rotated on use to reduce the impact of token theft.
  • Session invalidation. Sessions are invalidated on password change or account suspension.
  • Brute-force protection. Login attempts are rate-limited, with backoff and temporary lockout after repeated failures.

7. International Data Transfers

Personal data may be transferred to and processed in countries other than your country of residence, including India and the United States. When we transfer data internationally, we apply appropriate safeguards:

  • EU/EEA transfers: Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) approved by the European Commission.
  • UK transfers: UK International Data Transfer Agreement (IDTA) or SCCs with the UK Addendum.
  • India: Compliance with the India DPDP Act’s cross-border transfer provisions.
  • Brazil: Compliance with LGPD transfer requirements.
  • All third-party processors are contractually bound to maintain protection standards at least equivalent to those of the originating jurisdiction.

9. Incident Response

We maintain an incident response process to handle suspected or actual data security incidents. The process follows a standard sequence:

  • Detect. Monitoring of backend systems, authentication patterns, and unusual API activity.
  • Contain. Affected systems are isolated, compromised credentials rotated, and affected user sessions invalidated.
  • Eradicate. Root cause identified and remediated; vulnerabilities patched.
  • Notify. If an incident involves personal data and meets the notification threshold, affected users and regulators are notified without undue delay and, where applicable, within 72 hours in accordance with GDPR Article 33, the DPDP Act, and other applicable laws.
  • Recover. Services are restored and affected users supported with remediation steps.
  • Review. A post-incident review is conducted and lessons applied to prevent recurrence.

Backups & Disaster Recovery

  • Automated backups of backend data with the ability to restore to a recent point in time.
  • Backups are encrypted.
  • Restore procedures are tested periodically to verify that backups are valid.

10. Compliance

  • GDPR (EU/EEA): We process personal data in accordance with the GDPR, and our agreements with sub-processors include SCCs for cross-border transfers where applicable.
  • UK GDPR: Compliance maintained with UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018.
  • India DPDP Act, 2023: A Grievance Officer is appointed and contactable via email (see Section 13). Data Protection Board complaints are honored.
  • CCPA/CPRA (California): We do not sell personal information as defined by the CCPA/CPRA.
  • LGPD (Brazil): Compliance with LGPD obligations for users in Brazil.
  • Google Play policies: Our Data Safety declaration is completed and kept consistent with this policy, and an account deletion mechanism is provided in-app and documented on our website.
  • Security reviews: We conduct security reviews of the App and backend and address identified issues on a risk-prioritized basis.
  • Vulnerability management: Dependencies are reviewed regularly and security-relevant updates are applied promptly.

11. Your Rights as a Data Subject

You have specific rights regarding your personal data. Detailed coverage of these rights by jurisdiction is in our Privacy Policy, Section 6. The rights include:

  • Access — request a copy of the personal data we hold about you.
  • Correction — request correction of inaccurate or incomplete data.
  • Deletion — request erasure of your data.
  • Portability — receive your data in a structured, machine-readable format.
  • Restriction — request that we limit processing in certain circumstances.
  • Objection — object to processing based on legitimate interests.
  • Withdraw consent — where processing is based on consent.
  • Complaint — lodge a complaint with your local data protection authority.

To exercise any of these rights, contact us at support@plutontechnology.com. We respond within 30 days, or sooner if required by applicable law. We may request identity verification before fulfilling sensitive requests.

12. Responsible Disclosure

If you discover a security vulnerability in Orbit Scanner, we appreciate responsible disclosure. Please report it to developers@plutontechnology.com with:

  • A description of the vulnerability.
  • Steps to reproduce it.
  • Potential impact.
  • Your contact information.

We commit to:

  • Acknowledging receipt in a timely manner.
  • Working with you in good faith to validate and remediate the issue.
  • Crediting researchers (with permission) once the issue is resolved.
  • Not pursuing legal action against researchers who follow this responsible disclosure process.
